Here's the landscape of what "modern, full-stack" platforms serving your overlapping niches actually do — organized by category, with what's worth stealing for YBW.

## 1. Project/construction management suites (Procore, Buildertrend, Houzz Pro)
These target contractors and builders directly. Key moves: customer portals where clients can see schedule progress, approve selections, and sign change orders without a phone call, scheduling with dependency logic where finishing one task auto-adjusts dependent ones, and photo documentation organized by project and room. Estimating tools built for speed and repetition ideal for builders working on similar residential projects, with real-time job costing that tracks actuals against the original estimate.

**For YBW:** Your BOQ estimator (Phase 2) could add a lightweight "job costing" mode — once someone generates a BOQ, let them log actual spend against it as the build progresses. That's a small step from "estimator" to "tracker" and creates a reason to keep opening the app after the quote is done.

## 2. The Houzz model — inspiration + directory + marketplace in one
This is the closest analog to your "content moat + BOQ + marketplace" combo idea. Houzz is a two-sided marketplace connecting homeowners with home improvement professionals through design inspiration, professional directories, and integrated business management tools, and on the consumer side it operates a content and discovery platform centred on design inspiration, professional directories, product discovery, and homeowner engagement, while professionals get planning tools including 3D floor plans, takeoffs, contracts, bid management, scheduling, daily logs, procurement, estimates, invoices, and online payments.

**For YBW:** This validates your thesis almost exactly — content (inspiration) pulls in homeowners, the directory monetizes professionals, and the marketplace connects them. Houzz's "mood board / photo gallery" habit loop is worth copying cheaply: even a simple "save this finish/design" feature tied to your blog content turns passive readers into a warm lead list for your directory professionals.

## 3. BOQ/estimating tools (your direct competitors)
Addis Cost Estimator generates detailed Bills of Quantities in Excel format customized to location, with real-time material prices, labor costs, and equipment rates tailored to each Ethiopian city's market conditions, across Addis Ababa, Bahir Dar, Mekelle, Hawassa, Adama, and Jijiga. That's your actual bar to clear — multi-city, not just Addis.

**For YBW:** Two gaps to exploit — (1) they're a standalone tool, not bundled with content or marketplace, so there's no reason to stay after the BOQ is generated; (2) "Excel export" is table-stakes now — global tools (PlanSwift, CostX, Buildxact) differentiate on drawing takeoff (measuring quantities off an uploaded plan), which lines up exactly with your Phase 3 "digital takeoff" roadmap item. Getting there first in Ethiopia, even in a rough form, is a real moat.

## 4. Materials marketplace (B2B)
The pattern across serious players: calculators for bulk pricing, displays for technical specifications, and integration with suppliers, plus real-time inventory updates and automated pricing engines, and a reverse-RFQ model where buyers post specifications, quantities, and delivery deadlines and suppliers respond with bids — common in construction because orders are high-value, customized, or project-based.

**For YBW:** Given Ethiopia's cash-heavy, trust-driven B2B norms, a full self-serve checkout marketplace is premature. An RFQ/quote-request model ("post what you need, suppliers bid") fits Ethiopian relationship-based buying far better than a Shopify-style cart, and it's much cheaper to build. This also feeds your Telegram supplier-registration funnel directly — suppliers can quote via Telegram before you ever need a payment flow for them.

## What I'd actually prioritize
Given your zero-budget, solo-dev constraints and existing roadmap, in order of leverage:

1. **RFQ-style materials marketplace**, not catalog/checkout — matches local buying behavior and is far less engineering.
2. **"Save to my project" on content/blog** — turns YBW's bilingual content moat into a lead-gen engine for the directory and BOQ tool, Houzz-style.
3. **BOQ → actuals tracking** — cheap add-on to the existing estimator, extends session length and stickiness.
4. **Multi-city BOQ pricing** — closes the gap with Addis Cost Estimator directly.
5. **Digital takeoff (photo/plan upload → rough BOQ)** — your real long-term differentiator; even a crude MVP beats nothing, since no local competitor has it yet.

One caution from the local-market side: resist copying premium/subscription-first patterns (Houzz Pro's $99–399/month tiers) wholesale — Ethiopia's thin, price-sensitive market means your monetization should stay closer to your existing plan (low-price Telegram premium, commission-on-transaction) rather than SaaS-tier software fees, at least until you have real usage data.
